Regulation

Small homes and building permits in Slovakia

Slovakia's Building Act No. 25/2025 has applied since 1 April 2025. These are the real options — including the restriction the advertising tends to skip.

Legal routes for siting a small timber home in Slovakia
Route Limits Approval required The catch
Under 25 m² 25 m² built area, at least 2 m from the plot boundary None — no permit, no notification The structure must not be intended for housing or for business use. This is the most commonly missed point in the category.
Minor structure Single storey, up to 50 m² built area, up to 5 m high Notification to the building office. If it doesn't respond within 30 days, approval is deemed given. Must be ancillary to a main building on the plot.
Registered chassis Width ≤2.55 m, height ≤4 m, length typically 6–8 m Type approval / COC, registration (~€58 fee) and insurance Legally a vehicle, not a building. The housing and business restriction does not apply.
Full dwelling Any area, on foundations, with utility connections Building permit and occupancy approval Requires an A0 energy certificate plus fire, structural and hygiene compliance.

Our houses take the minor structure route. Footprints of 20, 30 and 39 m² — comfortably inside the 50 m² limit. This is not a permit process: you file a notification, and if the building office doesn't respond within 30 days, approval is deemed given. The documentation for that filing comes with every house, in the price.

Why 39 m² is more than it sounds. Footprint counts the ground the house stands on, not its floors. The show house has a 39 m² footprint and 65 m² of living space — ground floor plus a dormered upper floor.

Siting the house on a particular plot is the owner's responsibility — every plot carries different local rules, and we're not lawyers. We're happy to help you confirm your situation with the local building office before you order.

Construction

Timber frame, not sandwich panel

Most budget modules on the market are PIR sandwich panel. They're fast and cheap to make. We build a timber frame — and here's why.

Why timber

  • It breathes. A vapour-open build-up handles moisture better in year-round use.
  • It can be repaired. Replace a damaged section. A sandwich panel can't be repaired.
  • It lasts. A timber-framed house is built for decades, not for the length of the warranty.
  • It can be changed. A new window, a partition, a cable run — all still possible in ten years.
  • Carbon. Timber stores carbon. Polyurethane releases it.

FAQ

What people ask us most

Do I need a building permit?

Not a permit — a notification. Our models have footprints of 20, 30 and 39 m², inside the 50 m² minor structure limit. You file a notification with the building office, and if they don't respond within 30 days approval is deemed given. The Radhouses 20, sited at least 2 m from the boundary as an annexe to an existing house, needs not even that. Full detail is in the table above.

Can I live in it year-round?

Technically yes — insulation, triple glazing and heating are all specified for year-round use. Legally it's a different question: permanent residence in a full dwelling requires a building permit, occupancy approval and an A0 energy certificate. Most of our customers use the house as a holiday property, a studio or a letting unit, where those obligations don't arise. We'll work out which regime applies to your plans before you order.

How long does production take?

10 to 12 weeks from deposit, depending on the model and the season. Windows and solar have the longest lead times, so we order those immediately on signing.

How does payment work?

Three stages: 30% on order, 40% on completion of the structure, 30% on delivery. We don't start building before the deposit clears — and you don't pay the final third until the house is standing on your plot.

Do you deliver to Austria, Germany and Czechia?

Yes. Delivery beyond 150 km is charged per kilometre and quoted up front, not added to the invoice afterwards. We supply the technical documentation in English or German for export.

What's the warranty?

5 years on the structure and building envelope, 2 years on fittings and appliances. Every house has a photographic record of four build stages, so you know exactly what's inside the walls.
